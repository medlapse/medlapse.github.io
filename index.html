Are LLMs Up-to-date on Medical Knowledge?

Viet Pham1,  Jinjin Cai2,  Jing Su2,  Thai Le1
1Department of Computer Science, Indiana University  ·  2Indiana University School of Medicine

Biomedical evidence keeps changing: findings are revised, contradicted or overturned. Language models answer from what they memorised during pretraining, so they can keep giving the old answer. MedLapse measures this with 1,787 findings that their own authors corrected in later versions of medRxiv preprints. Each corrected finding is paired with an unchanged claim from the same paper, so a stale answer can be traced to stale memory rather than to a model that never saw the paper.

Across 16 open and commercial models, models pick the superseded finding in 30.3% of the lineages whose unchanged control they answer correctly, and the rate barely moves with scale, medical pretraining or training cutoff. Unlearning does not repair this. NPO and RMU reach 94–100% efficacy on the questions they are trained on, but the correction does not carry over to paraphrases of the same questions: there, RMU scores up to 6.1 points below the unedited model, and on findings fine-tuned into the model it reverses at most 23% of the stale shift.

1Motivation

A review of randomized trials in three leading journals counted 396 reversals of accepted practice. A model that was right on the day its training data was collected can therefore be confidently wrong today. We call this knowledge staleness: the model returns an answer that no longer matches the current evidence.

Why a stale answer is hard to diagnose

When a model gives the superseded answer, a standard benchmark cannot tell whether the model remembers the old finding or never saw the paper and guessed. Editing and unlearning inherit the same blind spot: removing a finding assumes the model holds it. MedLapse closes the gap by asking two questions about the same paper.

We tackle this problem by:

  1. Asking about an unchanged claim first.Each corrected finding is paired with a control question about a claim from the same paper that the authors never changed.
  2. Scoring only if the control is right.A wrong control answer gives no sign that the model learned the paper, so the pair is set aside. Only papers first posted before the model’s training cutoff are used.
  3. Then reading the revision answer.The four options are the current finding, the superseded finding and two distractors. Picking the superseded one counts as stale, the current one as current, a distractor as other.

2How MedLapse is built

When authors post a new version of a preprint, the difference between versions shows which findings changed and which did not. We extract claims from these papers and generate closed-book questions for significant revisions in three categories: epistemic nullification, where a finding loses or gains statistical significance; mechanism shift, where the proposed explanation changes; and direction flip, where the effect reverses. RQ1

How much superseded science do LLMs still hold?

Every lineage gives two probes with four options each: the revised finding and the paper’s unchanged control. A response counts as stale only when the paper’s first version predates the model’s cutoff, the model answers the control correctly, and it then picks the superseded finding.

Retention: share of lineages whose control claim yctl the model answers correctly.

Current: among those lineages, share answered with the current finding ycur.

Superseded: among those lineages, share answered with the superseded finding yprior (the staleness rate). Chance is 25%.

RQ2

Can machine unlearning correct stale knowledge?

We try forget-then-update on four open models with NPO and RMU under one objective that forgets yprior, learns ycur and retains the paper’s unchanged claims. Both methods reach 94–100% efficacy on the prompts they are trained on. Reworded versions of the same questions, and what the models actually write, tell a different story.

Trained prompts are fixed. On the pre-cutoff subset both methods make the current statement more likely than the superseded one on 94–100% of the prompts they are trained on.
−6.1

≤23%

NPO re-ranks without rewriting. After NPO, OLMo-3’s generations sit about as close to the superseded finding as to the current one (ROUGE-L 0.23 vs 0.27).
